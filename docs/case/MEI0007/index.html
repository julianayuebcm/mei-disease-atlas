What the papers report

3 reports
Janicic et al. 1995 · Am J Hum Genet · PMID 7717399 · coded from abstract
Insertion of an Alu sequence in the Ca(2+)-sensing receptor gene in familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia and neonatal severe hyperparathyroidism.
Coding disruption Antisense Alu inserted at codon 877 in exon 7 introduces stop codons in all reading frames, predicting a truncated CaR lacking most of the C-terminal intracellular domain. FHH carriers are heterozygous and NSHPT patients homozygous.
“Stop signals are introduced in all reading frames within the Alu sequence, leading to a predicted shortened mutant CaR protein.”
Bai et al. 1997 · J Clin Invest · PMID 9109436 · coded from abstract
Markedly reduced activity of mutant calcium-sensing receptor with an inserted Alu element from a kindred with familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia and neonatal severe hyperparathyroidism.
Coding disruption Alu inserted at codon 876 in exon 7 introduces stop codons in all three reading frames, producing receptors truncated by ~30 kD with no calcium response and reduced cell-surface expression; polymerase stalling at the Alu poly(T) tract was also seen in vitro.
“The insertion resulted in truncated receptor species that had molecular masses some 30 kD less than that of the wild-type CASR and exhibited no Ca2+(i) responses”
Cole et al. 1997 · Am J Med Genet · PMID 9217223 · coded from abstract
Neonatal severe hyperparathyroidism, secondary hyperparathyroidism, and familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia: multiple different phenotypes associated with an inactivating Alu insertion mutation of the calcium-sensing receptor gene.
Coding disruption An insertion containing an Alu element with an exceptionally long polyA tract in CASR exon 7 is described as inactivating; homozygotes have NSHPT and heterozygotes FHH. Molecular consequence beyond inactivation not described in the abstract.
“we found homoallelic homozygosity for an insertion mutation in exon 7 of CASR that includes an Alu repeat element with an exceptionally long polyA tract.”
