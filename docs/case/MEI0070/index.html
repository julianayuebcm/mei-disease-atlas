What the papers report

1 report
Yano et al. 2024 · J Med Genet · PMID 38960580 · coded from abstract
Long-read sequencing identifies an SVA_D retrotransposon insertion deep within the intron of ATP7A as a novel cause of occipital horn syndrome.
ExonizationAberrant splicing A 2.8 kb insertion deep within an ATP7A intron activates a new donor splice site located inside the element, producing aberrantly spliced mRNA. Long-read circular consensus sequencing identified the insertion as an evolutionarily old SVA_D. Antisense oligonucleotides against the new splice site restored normal transcript and functional ATP7A protein and reduced copper accumulation in patient fibroblasts; pedigree analysis dated the insertion to two generations earlier.
“This insertion caused aberrant mRNA splicing activated by a new donor splice site located within it.”
