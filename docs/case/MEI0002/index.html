Mechanism

Exonization primary

Sequence from the inserted element is spliced into the mRNA as a new exon or an exon extension.

Aberrant splicing

Other splice changes: cryptic splice sites, altered splice-site choice, pseudo-exons from flanking sequence.

Expression loss

Reduced or absent transcript or protein, without a more specific mechanism.

Coding disruption

The insertion lands in coding sequence and causes a frameshift, premature stop or disrupted protein.

What the papers report

15 reports
Taniguchi-Ikeda et al. 2011 · Nature · PMID 21979053 · coded from full text
Pathogenic exon-trapping by SVA retrotransposon and rescue in Fukuyama muscular dystrophy.
Coding disruptionExonizationAberrant splicing The ancestral SVA in the fukutin 3' UTR (exon 10) provides a strong splice acceptor that pairs with a rare alternative donor in exon 10, splicing out the 3' end of the coding region and proximal 3' UTR and adding SVA sequence; the protein loses its C-terminus and gains 129 SVA-encoded amino acids and mislocalizes. AONs against the SVA acceptor/ESE/ISE restored normal splicing and function.
“Sequence analysis revealed an abnormal splicing event, provoked by a strong acceptor site in SVA and a rare alternative donor site in fukutin exon 10.”
Kondo-Iida et al. 1999 · Hum Mol Genet · PMID 10545611 · coded from abstract
Novel mutations and genotype-phenotype relationships in 107 families with Fukuyama-type congenital muscular dystrophy (FCMD).
Not described Ancestral 3-kb retrotransposal insertion in the 3' non-coding region of FKTN found on 87% of FCMD chromosomes; the abstract does not name the element class or describe the molecular mechanism. Probands homozygous for it had milder phenotypes than compound heterozygotes with a point mutation.
“Most FCMD-bearing chromosomes examined to date (87%) have been derived from a single ancestral founder, whose mutation consisted of a 3 kb retrotransposal insertion in the 3' non-coding region of the fukutin gene.”
Toda et al. 1999 · J Mol Med (Berl) · PMID 10682317 · coded from abstract
Fukuyama-type congenital muscular dystrophy: the first human disease to be caused by an ancient retrotransposal integration.
Not described An ancient founder 3-kb retrotransposal insertion in the 3' non-coding region of FKTN accounts for 87% of FCMD chromosomes; element class and molecular mechanism are not described in the abstract.
“FCMD is the first human disease to be caused by an ancient retrotransposal integration.”
Kato et al. 2004 · Am J Med Genet A · PMID 15103718 · coded from abstract
A rapid diagnostic method for a retrotransposal insertional mutation into the FCMD gene in Japanese patients with Fukuyama congenital muscular dystrophy.
Not described Founder 3-kb retrotransposal insertion in the 3'UTR of FCMD; this paper describes a PCR diagnostic assay and does not investigate the mechanism or name the element class in the abstract. 16+3 homozygotes and 2 heterozygotes detected.
“In Japan, most FCMD-bearing chromosomes (87%) have a 3-kb retrotransposal insertion into the 3'-untranslated region (UTR) of the gene that could be derived from a single ancestral founder.”

An ancient retrotransposal insertion causes Fukuyama-type congenital muscular dystrophy.
Expression loss A ~3 kb retrotransposal insertion of tandemly repeated sequence (element class not named in the abstract) in the 3' UTR of fukutin is on all founder-haplotype FCMD chromosomes; the gene is expressed in controls but not in patients carrying the insertion.
“This gene is expressed in various tissues in normal individuals, but not in FCMD patients who carry the insertion.”
